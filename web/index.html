Get started

  1. Pair another machine

    Both machines run Sol. Once paired they can reach each other and send files.

  2. Add a service

    Tell Sol about an app on this machine, like a music or photo server.

  3. Share it with a peer

    On the Peers screen, switch on the services that machine may open.

Peers

Open on this machine

Peers' services kept reachable on this machine's own addresses. They stay open after a restart.

Leak check

Checks on your anonymous public services: things that could give away which machine serves them.

Recent activity

    Peers

    Other machines running Sol that you've paired with. Each side chooses what it shares, and either can send the other files.

    Services

    Apps and websites on this machine. Nobody can open one until you share it with a peer (Peers screen) or make it public.

    Files

    Files you add go on your shared files page, which a peer can open once you share it with them. Files peers send you land here too, marked with who sent them; only you see those.

    Log

    Pairings, connections, files and blocked attempts on this machine, newest first. Kept in memory: it starts again when the node restarts.

      Settings

        Help the Tor network

        Lets people in censored countries reach Tor through this machine. It needs no open port and is not a relay. While on, it contacts the Tor Project and public STUN servers from your IP address, and uses some of your bandwidth.

        Panel and node

        Every setting here is also in a terminal: sol config. Stop the node with sol stop, start it with sol.

        Sol

        Add a service

        Pick the app, or choose Custom. An app must already be running on this machine; a website is just a folder of files.

        Changes apply to new connections; ones already open keep going. A public service keeps its address. After a rename, peers keep their access but see the new name only when they refresh, and open it again under that name.
